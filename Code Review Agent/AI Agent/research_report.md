# Executive Brief: U.S. Waivers & Sanctions on Russian Oil

---

### Executive Summary

Following Russia’s 2022 invasion of Ukraine, the United States executed a dual-track energy strategy. While completely banning the domestic import of Russian energy, the U.S.—in coordination with the G7, EU, and Australia—established a **Price Cap Coalition**. 

Under this framework, **"waivers" (General Licenses)** allow Western maritime, insurance, and financial service providers to facilitate the transport of Russian oil to third countries, **provided it is purchased at or below specific price ceilings**. The goal is twofold: **constrain Kremlin revenue** while **preventing a global supply shock** that would spike worldwide inflation.

---

### Core Mechanism: The "Price Cap" Safe Harbor

The primary "waiver" mechanism is not a total exemption, but a conditional safe harbor administered by the U.S. Department of the Treasury’s **Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC)**.

| Category | Policy Rule | Price Threshold |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **U.S. Domestic Market** | Complete embargo on Russian crude and refined petroleum products. | **$0 / Prohibited** |
| **Crude Oil (Maritime)** | Western services permitted only if sold under the cap. | **$60 per barrel** |
| **High-Value Refined** | Western services permitted (e.g., Diesel, Gasoline, Kerosene). | **$100 per barrel** |
| **Low-Value Refined** | Western services permitted (e.g., Naphtha, Fuel Oil). | **$45 per barrel** |

---

### Key Specific Waivers & Carve-Outs

Beyond the general price-cap safe harbor, OFAC has issued specific General Licenses (GLs) to protect global energy stability and key allies:

*   **The Sakhalin-2 Project Carve-Out**
    *   **Beneficiary:** Japan.
    *   **Context:** Japan relies heavily on liquefied natural gas (LNG) and associated crude extraction from the Sakhalin-2 project in Russia's Far East.
    *   **Status:** OFAC consistently renews authorizations allowing the maritime transport and servicing of crude oil originating from this project, exempting it from the standard price cap.
*   **Emergency & Environmental Safety Exceptions**
    *   Waivers are granted for critical operations such as vessel crew safety, emergency port dockings, and the mitigation of environmental disasters (e.g., offshore oil spills).
*   **Non-Russian Origin Transits (CPC Blend & KEBCO)**
    *   Oil originating in Kazakhstan (such as Caspian Pipeline Consortium blend) that transits through Russian pipelines and ports is fully exempt from the cap, provided documentation proves non-Russian origin.
*   **Wind-Down Windows**
    *   Whenever new sanctions or vessel blacklists are introduced, OFAC typically issues temporary **45- to 90-day wind-down licenses** to allow maritime entities to offload cargoes already in transit without violating U.S. law.

### Strategic Objectives: The Policy Paradox

The design of the U.S. waiver framework balances two competing geopolitical priorities:

```
        THE DUAL-MANDATE STRATEGY
 ┌──────────────────────────────────────┐
 │  1. Curtail Kremlin War Financing    │
 │     Force Russia to sell oil at steep│
 │     discounts to non-Western buyers. │
 └──────────────────┬───────────────────┘
                    │
                    ▼
 ┌──────────────────────────────────────┐
 │  2. Preserve Global Market Liquidity │
 │     Avoid taking ~7M bpd of Russian  │
 │     oil off the market, preventing a │
 │     catastrophic spike in crude.     │
 └──────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Evolution & Enforcement (2023–Present)

The waiver system has evolved from a relatively permissive honor system into a tightly enforced regime:

1.  **The Rise of the "Shadow Fleet":** Russia bypassed Western services by purchasing hundreds of aging, non-Western insured tankers to sell crude above the $60 cap—primarily to India and China.
2.  **Enforcement Crackdown (Phase 2):** Starting in late 2023, the U.S. shifted tactics. Instead of altering the price threshold, OFAC began aggressively sanctioning individual shipping companies, traders, and specific vessels carrying oil sold above the cap.
3.  **Heightened Due Diligence:** Maritime service providers (P&I clubs, shipbrokers) are now required to collect itemized ancillary costs (shipping, insurance, freight) to prevent traders from hiding above-cap crude prices inside inflated transit fees.

---

### Summary Takeaway

U.S. Russian oil waivers are **not concessions**, but rather a **deliberate economic valve**. They enable non-G7 nations to buy discounted Russian oil using Western insurance and shipping infrastructure—deliberately keeping the global market supplied while forcing Moscow to bear the cost of steep discounts, inefficient shadow logistics, and reduced tax revenues.
